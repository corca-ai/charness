# Claim Boundary Frame

Apply this frame before editing a requested mechanism and before reporting that its outcome succeeded:

1. **Concept and premise** — name the actor's job and why this mechanism is necessary. If the requested distinction already has only one state, the premise is false; for example, a `--json` choice adds no capability when output is always structured JSON.
2. **Consumer and state** — name the real consumer and the observable difference it must see. A field, option, marker, or check with no consumer or no distinct state is not justified by its shape.
3. **Behavior owner** — follow the behavior to its canonical product boundary. A fixture or simulation may own an honestly labelled lower-level claim, but it cannot replace that boundary or be promoted into installed, live, provider, or release evidence.
4. **Evidence level** — state the level being claimed and name an observer that can distinguish success from failure at that level, including the concrete falsifying observation. Source inspection, a fixture, and a simulated run remain valid only for the levels they can observe.

Proceed when the four answers align. If the premise is false, the actual owner lies outside the write scope, or the available observer cannot see the claimed level, do not add a substitute path. Narrow the claim or escalate with `BLOCKED: premise/scope mismatch - <concrete reason>` before editing. This is a judgment frame, not a new artifact, checklist gate, or ban on lower-level tests.

## Expert Counterfactuals

**Engelbart, `system-improving-itself` (H + LAM + T as one unit).** I improved
the LAM (the detector, the tests, the checklist text) and left T — the
tool-system that decides *when* review happens — unchanged, so the same defect
class had to be caught by a human-shaped process five times. The Engelbart move
is to notice that "the repair carries its class" is now measured at 5/5 and
therefore belongs in T, not in advice: a slice touching verdict logic should
route the *repair* to a reviewer automatically, not because I remembered to. The
two-round cap currently spends round 2 on the repair and then stops precisely
when the measured hit rate says a third would pay.

**Gary Klein, pre-mortem.** Before the `<plugin-dir>` sweep, the question "if
this rewrite breaks something, what will it be?" has one obvious answer — a
string that is executed rather than displayed — and that answer was already
written down twice in this repo (`EXECUTED_COMMAND_FIELDS`, and the
`executed-vs-read-field` lesson on my own session-open list). A 30-second
pre-mortem before a blanket regex would have converted a review round into a
grep. The generalizable form: **a bulk rewrite of command-shaped strings owes a
pre-mortem naming the executor**, because the read/run distinction is invisible
in the value.

itself was cheap. What cost was that the FIRST draft carried four factual

Not waste: the three reviewer spawns. They are the reason the artifact is worth
activating, and the alternative — activating a goal whose acceptance passes at

## Expert Counterfactuals

**Engelbart (system-improving-itself) — treat (H + LAM + T) as one unit.**
I improved the METHOD (the goal artifact now demands a numeric floor, negative
controls, a three-valued verdict) and left the TOOL untouched. The four false
premises and the three carried-class repairs were all found by human-shaped
reading — a reviewer with grep. Engelbart's move is to ask what T would have to
exist for the LAM to hold without the reviewer: a claim-checking pass over an
artifact's `file:line` citations, which is mechanical (every one of my wrong
citations was a `path:line` that did not say what the sentence claimed) and
which no gate in this repo performs. That is a smaller, more buildable tool than
the semantic detector slice 1 proposes, and it would have caught four of the
nine blockers. The goal I just shaped aims T at code diffs and leaves its own
artifact class unserved.

**Second lens — Gary Klein (pre-mortem), on decision quality.**
I asked the operator four framing questions and got four good answers, then
wrote the artifact and asked reviewers to find what was wrong. A pre-mortem
would have inverted the cheapest one: "this goal was reported complete and the
detector catches nothing — how?" That is precisely the round-1 finding, and it
is answerable in two minutes by the author, before a reviewer is spawned. The
counterfactual is not "review less" — it is that the FIRST question about an
acceptance list should be how it passes while failing, and I asked it only by
delegating it.

- **Cross-repo/portable**: general to any repo whose agents write artifacts

## Portable Candidate

Abstract pattern: a cheap citation-liveness check over agent-authored artifacts
— for each `path:line` a durable artifact asserts, does the path exist, does the
line exist, and does it contain the identifier the surrounding sentence names.
Deliberately syntactic; it does not judge whether the claim is true.

Triggering evidence: nine citation defects in one shaping session, four of them
load-bearing on a design decision, all found by human reading.

Intended consumer shape: any repo where agents write durable artifacts that cite
source locations — which is every repo `charness` targets.

Destination: **candidate, gated on the reconciliation with slice 1.** Both this
and the successor goal's slice 1 read a diff/artifact and ask "does the cited
thing contain what the text claims". Shipping both without deciding whether they
are one engine would be `one-engine-per-pattern` at the tooling layer.

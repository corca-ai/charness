# Issues 460 / 461 / 463 — binding scope, review-window attribution, derived-artifact inventory
Date: 2026-07-27

## Decision Under Review

Three backlog issues closed in one slice.

**#460.** The reviewed-input identity is now content-addressed under `sha256-v2`:
in working-tree mode the digest covers the declared paths and their bytes plus
exec bit, while `base_head`, the staged/unstaged patch digests,
`declared_untracked`, and `auto_excluded_paths` stay as provenance. `verify`
recomputes under the algorithm recorded in the packet, so v1 bindings keep v1
rules. The auto sweep drops everything under the critique adapter `output_dir`,
so authoring the record cannot stale the binding that describes it; explicit
`--reviewed-path` still wins.

**#461.** `reviewer_boundary_fingerprint.py` snapshots per-path content digests,
stamps a review-window id that `verify` refuses to answer across, and accepts
kind-scoped parent declarations (`--parent-path`, `--parent-staged`,
`--parent-head-moved`) so drift the parent caused is reported instead of failing
as an unattributable boundary alarm. State capture split into
`reviewer_boundary_state.py` under the length cap.

**#463.** Inventory of which foreign-writable artifacts are purely derived and
ungated, recorded as an audit artifact, with the shape-only contract stated in
`docs/artifact-policy.md`.

Out of scope: adding recompute gates to partly-derived artifacts, and wiring the
shape-only contract into `.agents/surfaces.json` entries.

## Failure Angles

- **Binding correctness (#460).** Does v2 still stale for every change a reviewer
  would care about, is the v1/v2 dispatch downgrade-proof, and does the prefix
  exclusion over-match siblings?
- **Enforcement weakening (#461).** Is parent attribution net worse than the
  status quo it replaces, and can a declaration excuse a real violation?
- **Inventory truth (#463).** Is any writer missing, is any "partly derived"
  classification hiding an actionable gap, and does the policy text overclaim?

## Counterweight Pass

The angles are not symmetric in what they proved. The #460 and #461 reviewers
each surfaced two capability regressions I had introduced and did not see; the
#463 reviewer refuted a claim I had written into the policy doc. All three were
acted on rather than argued down. What I did reject: the suggestion to make the
aggregate patch backstop unconditional in `verify` — a declared parent edit always
moves the aggregate digest, so an always-on pathless entry (never attributable by
construction) would fail every attributed verify and re-create the exact
discount-the-alarm dynamic #461 exists to end. Per-path content digests fix the
same hole without that cost.

## Deliberately Not Doing

- No unconditional aggregate patch check in `verify`: it would fail every attributed run by construction and rebuild the discount-the-alarm dynamic.
- No recompute gates for the packet or release-ledger families: their bytes carry timestamps and subprocess output, so a byte compare fires on every re-run and proves nothing.
- No `.agents/surfaces.json` edits recording shape-only contracts: real (F14), but an inventory pass should not silently become a surface-contract change.
- No mandatory `--window-id`: it would break verification against snapshots written by an older copy, which is the state every installed consumer is in today.

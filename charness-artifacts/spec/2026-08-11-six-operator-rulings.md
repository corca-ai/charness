# Six operator rulings — 2026-08-11

Date: 2026-08-11. Six decisions the 2026-08-11 triage queued as operator-reserved
were taken in session. Each carries the evidence that produced it, because four of
the six overturned the framing the triage handed up.

## The class three of them share

`#1`, `#2`, and `#6` are one defect wearing three costumes: **an identifier
hand-transcribed from a source that could have produced it.**

| Ruling | Transcribed by hand | Authority that already knows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | retired CLI names in `deprecated_aliases` | `charness --help` (argparse) |
| 2 | D47's published figures | the probe JSON |
| 6 | `<test_file>::<target>` path pairs | the call site's own content |

The repo already names this class — `regenerable-facts` in
[check_doc_authoring_preflight.py](../../scripts/check_doc_authoring_preflight.py) — and
its rule reaches none of the three. `#4` is the same axis inverted: a proposed
deprecation window would have added a rule to substitute for a channel (release notes)
that already works.

## 1. `#598` — replace `domain_language_contract`, then delete it

**Status: EXECUTED.** Derived check first, then the deletion, in that binding order.
Executing it corrected the sizing below: one live defect and one false positive. The gate's
module docstring carries the three limits on the word "replaces".

**Ruling:** build a derived check that every documented `charness <subcommand>` resolves
to a real subcommand, then delete `domain_language_contract` as a capability — script,
public adapter-contract section, catalog, inventory-dispatch, consumer-fields, the
[quality adapter](../../.agents/quality-adapter.yaml) block, three runtime budgets, the queue entry, the timing
row, three test files, and the dogfood claim. Concept vocabulary (`support skill` vs
`support capability`) is absorbed into `#599`, not gated.

**Why the issue's own framing was wrong.** `#598` calls it a word preference on markdown
prose, and under that framing north-star P1 forbids the teeth. But only ONE of three
declared terms carries `deprecated_aliases`, and all three of its strings are retired CLI
invocation forms — over a scan scope that includes `skills/public/**/*.md`, the export
source for the shipped plugin. That is P5 form-and-shipped-surface, not preference.

**Why replace rather than keep.** A hand-declared alias list cannot catch a rename nobody
declared, which is the only failure that actually reaches a consumer. Derivation catches
it and needs no list. `check_documented_command_flags.py` already argues exactly this for
FLAGS — "an argparse contract rather than another literal" — and stops one rung short.

**Order is binding:** the derived check lands and works BEFORE the deletion, so no
interval exists in which a retired command can ship unchallenged.

## 2. `#596` — stamp D47, do not extend the detector

**Status: not executed.**

**Ruling:** date-and-hash-stamp D47's measurement (headline figures keep a date, the rest
becomes a probe link plus `sha256`), let the probe become an immutable dated snapshot, and
convert the equality pins to invariants matching
[probe_drift_support.py](../../tests/probe_drift_support.py). **Do NOT extend
`regenerable-facts` to decision documents.**

**Why the stamp.** The pin exists because D47 states a measurement in the present tense.
Stamped, corpus growth cannot falsify it, so nothing needs forcing and the probe stops
being regenerated by unrelated work. The repo already ships this exact shape: the handoff's
`charness-publish-state-claim` block carries `manifest_path` + `manifest_sha256` +
`captured_at`.

**Why NOT extend the detector — measured, not argued.** Run against D47's own text, the
`regenerable-facts` count rule fires on 4 of ~19 published figures, and all four are
`N artifacts` denominator fragments. Every headline figure the pin exists to protect —
196, 188, 153, 35 — passes clean, because measurement prose puts the number last
("the presence-only mention total is **196**") or uses a noun outside the closed
ten-noun list ("261 floor-clearing **mentions**"). Widening the list trades false
negatives for false positives on ordinary prose, which the rule's own comment says. A 20%
detector for a problem the stamp removes by construction is not worth its false positives.

## 3. `check-timing-layer-completeness` — CI step, not a pre-push label

**Status: not executed.**

**Ruling:** add a step to [quality-core.yml](../../.github/workflows/quality-core.yml). Do
not add the label to `.githooks/pre-push` `DOCS_ONLY_LABELS`.

**Why the triage's premise was false.** It reported no enforcement locally or in CI. The
gate DOES run at commit time
([staged_commit_gate_plan.py](../../scripts/staged_commit_gate_plan.py)), triggered by
exactly the two files that can flip its verdict. Adding the pre-push label would duplicate
that, unscoped, in a lane this repo keeps fast.

**Where the real hole is.** Hooks are opt-in (`core.hooksPath`), and CI runs a hand-picked
subset that omits this gate. So an unhooked contributor, a web-UI edit, or `--no-verify`
reaches nothing. Precedent: the `check-plugin-doc-links` row records a round-2 review
ruling that "claiming CI without a CI step is the same overclaim one level up", and a CI
step was added for it.

## 4. Deprecated shipped surfaces — no window; the release notes carry it

**Status: partially executed before this ruling.** `check_title_slug_drift` was already
deleted at `afaaef4e`; what remains owed is the release-note line at the next bump.

**Ruling:** no time-based or release-count deprecation window. Remove the surface and name
the removal in the release notes at a version bump. Delete
`check_title_slug_drift` (four paths plus its title-slug retirement compatibility test under
`<authoring-repo>/tests/quality_gates/`, both since deleted at `afaaef4e`) and reserve the note
line; the removal of a shipped entrypoint is a major bump.

**Operator decision (2026-08-12):** a portable replacement, compatibility promise, or
migration path is not a precondition for removing a consumer-facing feature. The release
note must identify what was removed and why. Existing removal-consumer verification and
version-boundary policy still apply; this decision does not claim that the next release-note
line has already been published.

**Why no window.** A window substitutes for communication this repo already does. Release
notes lead with breaking changes, identify the changed surface and its reason, and give a
remedy when one exists — v4.2.0 opens "Read this first: three floors can now refuse work that
previously passed" and tells the reader what to add to their adapter. Semver is used for
real, `compute_update_available` tells consumers an update exists, and they update
deliberately via `charness update`. The script also already prints its own `DEPRECATED:`
notice when run, so the channel is doubled.

**The counter-evidence that killed the window.** Release cadence is v3.4.0 8/6, v3.5.0 8/7,
v4.0.0 8/9, v4.1.0 8/10 01:05, v4.2.0 8/10 07:36. "Two minor releases" is six and a half
hours here, so a consumer updating weekly would never see the notice — a release-count
window is a false comfort, and a time window is a rule invented to distrust a channel that
works. If a removal cannot be explained in the notes, that is a signal to write more notes.

## 5. Judge-intent Cautilus scenario — build it and run it once

**Status: not executed.**

**Ruling:** build a scenario whose prompt does not determine the route, asserting
`engage-always` on `workflow-trigger.md` and `state-selection.md`; then run Cautilus once
(ask before running). Two of three allowlist waivers go stale-advisory. The third
(`adapter-contract.md`, an unhealthy-adapter fixture) is not dischargeable this way and
stays.

**What the branch is.** `judge_from_user_request` is where the planner DECLINES to guess
and forces the two documents needed to judge instead. It is the safety net under the whole
"declare your routing" contract — the branch that runs when the agent is least sure — and
no registered scenario exercises it. Both waivers say so in their own text and name this
discharge.

**Honest limit:** Cautilus is ask-before-run, so adding the scenario converts "waived
prose" into "a runnable, gradable claim", not into "verified". The single run is what makes
it verified, which is why the ruling includes it.

## 6. `#585` — content-fingerprint re-key

**Status: not executed.** The largest slice, and the only schema change.

**Ruling:** re-key the boundary-bypass identity arm on normalized call-site content,
following the [nose fingerprint](../../skills/public/quality/scripts/nose_fingerprint_lib.py)
contract: path-invariant, content/membership/multiplicity-sensitive, with the algorithm
version stamped into the baseline. **Do not `set()`-dedup the member hashes.**

**This fix already happened once, on the descendant.** The boundary-bypass ratchet was
built 2026-06-03 with path-pair keys; the dup ratchet — which the audit records as citing
boundary-bypass as its own design template — hit the same defect and re-keyed to content
fingerprints on 2026-06-27 ("resolves D30"). The template never received its student's
lesson. This ruling propagates D30 back.

**Interaction with this session's narrowing, stated plainly.** `candidate_key_count` left
`COUNT_FIELDS` at `322664d5`, which removes one accidental backstop for a colliding content
key. nose's contract answers that directly — preserve multiplicity in the key rather than
lean on a count field — so the narrowing is harmless under this ruling and load-bearing
under no other.

**`#585`'s internal open decision is discharged** by ruling 2: it asked whether `#561`'s
equality pins should become invariants. They should.

## Feasibility already measured

**Ruling 1 is DESIGNED, not just decided.** Scanning shipped prose for
`charness <token>`:

| Scan scope | Non-subcommand hits |
| --- | --- |
| all prose | 72 across 36 tokens (`charness itself`, `charness does`, `charness ships`…) |
| code contexts only | 27 across 10 |
| shell-language fences + inline code | **3** |

Of the final three, one is a comment line inside a fence (skip comment lines) and **two are
real**: `charness verify`
([maintainer-local-enforcement.md:192](../../skills/public/quality/references/maintainer-local-enforcement.md))
and `charness propose`
([mutation-testing.md:166](../../skills/public/quality/references/mutation-testing.md))
name commands that do not exist and never did. The check finds two live defects on its
first run. `argparse_surface_lib` already parses `--help`, and `charness --help` exposes
all thirteen subcommands.

**Ruling 6 is the largest slice.**
[inventory_boundary_bypass_lib.py](../../scripts/inventory_boundary_bypass_lib.py) walks
AST call nodes but records NO line information, and `import_safe_targets` is a flat string
list per row. Content fingerprinting needs per-call-site spans, so the payload schema, the
public payload validator, and both baselines all move together. `lineno`/`end_lineno` are
free on every `ast.Call`; the cost is the schema change, not the extraction.
